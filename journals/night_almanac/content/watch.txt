I have kept the watch for three years now and I no longer sleep in the thirteenth month. There are three of them that wander. The others keep their places as stars should, but these three go round the pale one in the middle like dogs about a table, each at its own pace, and none of them keeps step with the year. I thought at first my eyes were failing. I thought the glass was flawed. I ground a new glass and they had moved again. The nearest goes round in five months. I have marked its houses on the wall and on my arm and in the margin of every page so that I will not be told later that I imagined it. The second takes seven. The third takes eight, and it is the third I like least, because it weeps, and what it weeps does not fall but hangs there under it as if the dark were a sheet of glass and something on the other side were leaning its face against it. Each year on the same night I climb to the roof and I set down where they stand. Each year they have crept on. The first gains three houses in a year. The second loses one. The third gains five. I did not see it for a long time and then I saw nothing else. They are not wandering. They are being brought round. There is one line in that sky, one only, on which all three can stand together with the pale one, and on the night they stand on it there will be a straight road from the middle of things down to the roof of this house. I have reckoned the night. I reckoned it four times with four different pens and it came out the same and I burned the papers and I have reckoned it again here in the old hand where the others will not think to look. The innkeeper says the lake was higher when he was a boy. He says there were towers on the far shore once and that on still nights you could see them twice, once standing up and once hanging down in the water, and that it was the ones in the water that had lights in them. He laughs when he says it. He has stopped going out after dark. Nobody here wears a mask at the festival any longer and nobody will tell me why they stopped. I found one in the loft, white, with the paint coming off it in leaves, and I have hung it facing the wall. Count the houses. Count them again. When the three are in their first houses the road is open and it does not matter then whether I am watching.
