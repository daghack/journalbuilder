The mere lies black below the hall, and it does not move. Wind comes over the fen and lays the reeds flat, and the water does not stir; the moon lies on it whole, as on a plate of glass. My father kept it, and his father, and none of us has known its bottom. I have let the line down sixty fathoms and felt nothing take it, and drawn it up cut clean, as by a blade. The knives we keep are black glass, struck from the stones of the shore. My father said steel will not cut what comes up out of it, and the black will. I did not believe him until the winter the hounds would not go down to the water. They lay in the yard with their hackles up and watched the mere all night, and in the morning there were prints on the shore going in, and none coming out. Since then I have walked the bank at night with a blade in each hand. Something hunts there. I hear it in the reeds, keeping pace with me, and when I stop it stops. Twice I have seen the water open without a sound and close again, and the moon lie on it whole as before. Under the surface there are shapes that turn slowly, larger than the hall, and they are not fish. Seven have gone out on the mere since Michaelmas and I have written them in the book with the rest. None of them came back. I keep the guard of my grandfather's sword, which is pierced, and the line, and the board we reckon soundings by. If I do not come back, the book will tell you what I found, if you can read it. Do not go down to the water at night. Do not answer if you hear your name from the reeds.
