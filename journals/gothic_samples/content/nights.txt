Since the equinox I have dreamt the same dream, and I set it down here because the doctor says to, and because I no longer trust myself to remember what is mine. I am on a heath at night. There is no moon. There is a door standing alone in the heather, black, in a frame of grey stone, and nothing behind it but more heath. It has no knob and no keyhole and no hinges that I can find. Above it the stars are too many and too near, and they are not stars. They open. They watch me cross the heath and they watch the door, and every night the three that wander have moved a little nearer to standing in one line above it. I knock. Nothing answers. I wake with my knuckles bruised. Then the mirrors began. In the glass over the mantel, behind my own face, there was the heath, and the door, small and black, as if it stood in the room behind me. I turned and there was only the wallpaper. The girl will not dust the long glasses in the gallery now; she says the lady in them does not move when she moves. I have covered the mirrors in my room with sheets, as they do in a house where someone has died. It does not help. The window at night is a mirror too, and so is the black of the water in the basin, and so are the eyes of the cat. Last night something sat on my chest while I slept, and I could not move or cry out, and over its shoulder I saw the door, and it stood open a finger's width, and there was light behind it. I have learned the stars' houses from the almanac. I have written what the glasses said, backwards, as they said it. I have counted the knocks. Three, and three, and three, and none of them mine. If I am right, the door opens only from the other side, and whoever is knocking is already in the house. Do not look for me in the glass. Do not wake me if I walk.
